How to Calculate Your Colorado Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract Colorado's $16,100 standard deduction from gross wages, then apply the flat 4.40% state rate.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Colorado resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. Colorado state tax: ($65,000 - $16,100 deduction) × 4.40% = $2,151.6
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $12,744.1 = $52,255.9 (≈$4,354.66/month)

Colorado Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What is Colorado's state income tax rate?

Colorado charges a flat 4.40% rate for tax year 2026. Standard deduction: $16,100 for single filers.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Colorado?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Colorado takes home roughly $52,255.9/year (about $4,354.66/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax.

What deductions does Colorado apply before taxing my income?

Colorado applies a $16,100 standard deduction, before the flat 4.40% rate. See the "How Colorado Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and Colorado state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Colorado Department of Revenue's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.

Methodology & Source

Colorado state tax figures sourced from Colorado Department of Revenue (https://tax.colorado.gov/), citing Colo. Const. art. X (flat rate mandate) — 4.4% per Proposition 121 (2022). Federal brackets, standard deductions (single/MFJ/HoH), and FICA constants sourced from the IRS (Revenue Procedure 2025-32; married-filing-jointly Additional Medicare threshold of $250,000 is a separate, unindexed statutory figure). Colorado's married-filing-jointly and head-of-household figures are not yet independently verified and currently fall back to single-filer brackets — see the caveat in the formula section above.

This is an estimate based on standard single-filer federal, FICA, and CO state calculations. Actual withholding may differ based on your W-4 elections, pre-tax deductions, and other factors. Colorado starts from federal taxable income; this estimate applies the federal standard deduction as the base. Colorado's TABOR mechanism can temporarily lower the effective rate in high-revenue years, not modeled here.
