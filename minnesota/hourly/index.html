How to Calculate Your Minnesota Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract Minnesota's $14,575 standard deduction from gross wages, then apply the 4-bracket state schedule (5.35%-9.85%) marginally.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Minnesota resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. Minnesota state tax: Marginal tax on $50,425 taxable income (after $14,575 deduction) = $2,969.4
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $13,561.9 = $51,438.1 (≈$4,286.51/month)

Minnesota Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What are Minnesota's income tax brackets for 2026?

Minnesota uses 4 marginal brackets ranging from 5.35% to 9.85%. Standard deduction: $14,575 for single filers.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Minnesota?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Minnesota takes home roughly $51,438.1/year (about $4,286.51/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax.

What deductions does Minnesota apply before taxing my income?

Minnesota applies a $14,575 standard deduction, before its marginal bracket schedule. See the "How Minnesota Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and Minnesota state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Minnesota Department of Revenue's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.
