How to Calculate Your Delaware Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract Delaware's $3,250 standard deduction from gross wages, then apply the 7-bracket state schedule (0%-6.60%) marginally.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Delaware resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. Delaware state tax: Marginal tax on $61,750 taxable income (after $3,250 deduction) = $3,059
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $13,651.5 = $51,348.5 (≈$4,279.04/month)

Delaware Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What are Delaware's income tax brackets for 2026?

Delaware uses 7 marginal brackets ranging from 0% to 6.60%. Standard deduction: $3,250 for single filers.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Delaware?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Delaware takes home roughly $51,348.5/year (about $4,279.04/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax.

What deductions does Delaware apply before taxing my income?

Delaware applies a $3,250 standard deduction, before its marginal bracket schedule. See the "How Delaware Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and Delaware state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Delaware Division of Revenue's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.
