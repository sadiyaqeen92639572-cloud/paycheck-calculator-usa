How to Calculate Your Ohio Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single), apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to $184,500) + 1.45% Medicare (+0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Ohio taxes the first $26,050 of income at 0%, then a flat 2.75% on everything above that — no separate standard deduction to subtract first.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay before any municipal income tax.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Ohio resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $65,000 × 7.65% = $4,972.50
  3. OH state tax: ($65,000 - $26,050) × 2.75% = $38,950 × 2.75% = $1,071.13
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $5,620 - $4,972.50 - $1,071.13 = $53,336.37 (≈$4,444.70/month, before municipal tax)

Ohio Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What is Ohio's state income tax rate in 2026?

Ohio completed a multi-year phase-down to a single flat rate for 2026: 0% on the first $26,050 of nonbusiness income, then 2.75% on everything above that — down from 3.125% in 2025 and 3.5% in 2024.

Does this calculator include Ohio city/municipal income tax?

No — and this matters a lot in Ohio. Over 600 Ohio municipalities levy their own income tax, typically 1%-3%, with Columbus, Cleveland, and Akron around 2.5%. For many Ohio taxpayers, city tax exceeds state tax. Check your city's rate at your municipality's finance department or RITA/CCA (Ohio's two major municipal tax collection agencies) — it is not included in this estimate.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Ohio?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Ohio takes home roughly $53,368/year (about $4,447/month) after federal tax, FICA, and Ohio's 2.75% state tax — before any city income tax, which can add another 1%-3%.
