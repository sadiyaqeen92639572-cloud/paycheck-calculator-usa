How to Calculate Your Illinois Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single), apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to $184,500) + 1.45% Medicare (+0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract Illinois's $2,425 personal exemption from gross wages, then apply the flat 4.95% state rate to the remainder.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay — Illinois has no local income tax.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Illinois resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $65,000 × 7.65% = $4,972.50
  3. IL taxable wages: $65,000 - $2,425 = $62,575 → IL tax: $62,575 × 4.95% = $3,097.46
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $5,620 - $4,972.50 - $3,097.46 = $51,310.04 (≈$4,275.84/month)

Illinois Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What is Illinois's state income tax rate?

Illinois charges a flat 4.95% income tax rate on all taxable income — the Illinois Constitution (art. IX, § 3) prohibits graduated rates. Each filer gets a $2,425 personal exemption in 2026.

Does Illinois tax retirement income?

No — Illinois does not tax Social Security, pension income, or 401(k)/IRA withdrawals, making it comparatively favorable for retirees despite the flat wage tax.

Does Illinois have local income tax?

No. Unlike Pennsylvania or Ohio, no Illinois city or county adds a separate local income tax on top of the state's flat 4.95% rate.
