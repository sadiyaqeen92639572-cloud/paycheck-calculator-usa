How to Calculate Your Arkansas Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract Arkansas's apply the 5-bracket state schedule (0%-3.70%) marginally to gross wages.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Arkansas resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. Arkansas state tax: Marginal tax on $65,000 taxable income (after $0 deduction) = $2,037.84
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $12,630.34 = $52,369.66 (≈$4,364.14/month)

Arkansas Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What are Arkansas's income tax brackets for 2026?

Arkansas uses 5 marginal brackets ranging from 0% to 3.70%.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Arkansas?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Arkansas takes home roughly $52,369.66/year (about $4,364.14/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax.

What deductions does Arkansas apply before taxing my income?

Arkansas applies no standard deduction — the full rate applies to gross wages, before its marginal bracket schedule. See the "How Arkansas Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and Arkansas state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Arkansas Department of Finance and Administration's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.
