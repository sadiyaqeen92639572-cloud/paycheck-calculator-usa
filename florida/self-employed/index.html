Florida Paycheck Calculator FAQ

Does Florida have a state income tax?

No. Florida's constitution (art. VII, § 5) has barred a state personal income tax since 1924. Only federal income tax and FICA are withheld from Florida paychecks.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Florida?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Florida takes home roughly $54,440/year (about $4,537/month) after federal income tax and FICA — no state tax is withheld.

Why does Florida have no income tax?

Florida funds state government primarily through sales tax and tourism-related revenue instead of a personal income tax, a structure protected by its state constitution.

Methodology & Source

Florida state tax figures sourced from Florida Department of Revenue (https://floridarevenue.com/), citing Fla. Const. art. VII, § 5 (constitutional ban on a state income tax). Federal brackets, standard deductions (single/MFJ/HoH), and FICA constants sourced from the IRS (Revenue Procedure 2025-32; married-filing-jointly Additional Medicare threshold of $250,000 is a separate, unindexed statutory figure). Florida's married-filing-jointly and head-of-household figures have been independently verified against the source above.

Self-employment tax uses the statutory 15.3% rate (12.4% Social Security + 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net self-employment income, with half of the SE tax deducted from federal taxable income above-the-line — per IRS Schedule SE (Form 1040) instructions, IRC §1401 and §164(f), 2026 edition. The 20% Qualified Business Income deduction (IRC §199A) is not modeled — it phases out by income and business type and is too state/entity-dependent to compute generically. This assumes the self-employment income is your only earnings for the year; if you also have W-2 wages, the Social Security wage-base cap and Additional Medicare threshold are actually based on your combined earnings, so this will overstate SE tax if you have significant wage income too.

This is an estimate based on standard single-filer federal and FICA calculations. Actual withholding may differ based on your W-4 elections, pre-tax deductions (401k, health insurance), and other factors.
