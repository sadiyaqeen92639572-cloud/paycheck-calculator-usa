Texas Paycheck Calculator FAQ

Does Texas have a state income tax?

No. Texas is one of nine states with no personal income tax, a status enshrined in the Texas Constitution since a 2019 voter-approved amendment (art. VIII, § 24). Your paycheck only has federal income tax and FICA withheld.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Texas?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Texas takes home roughly $54,440/year (about $4,537/month) after federal income tax and FICA — no state tax is withheld.

Are there any hidden state or local income taxes in Texas?

No Texas city or county levies a local income tax. Texas instead relies more heavily on sales tax and property tax to fund state and local government.

Methodology & Source

Texas state tax figures sourced from Texas Comptroller of Public Accounts (https://comptroller.texas.gov/), citing Tex. Const. art. VIII, § 24 (voter-approved 2019 ban on a state personal income tax). Federal brackets, standard deductions (single/MFJ/HoH), and FICA constants sourced from the IRS (Revenue Procedure 2025-32; married-filing-jointly Additional Medicare threshold of $250,000 is a separate, unindexed statutory figure). Texas's married-filing-jointly and head-of-household figures have been independently verified against the source above.

Bonus federal withholding uses the flat supplemental-wage rate (22%, 37% above $1,000,000 cumulative supplemental wages/year), per IRS Publication 15 (Circular E), Employer's Tax Guide, 2026 edition — the alternative "aggregate method" is not modeled.

This is an estimate based on standard single-filer federal and FICA calculations. Actual withholding may differ based on your W-4 elections, pre-tax deductions (401k, health insurance), and other factors.
