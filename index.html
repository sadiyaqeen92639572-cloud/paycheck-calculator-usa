How Your Paycheck Is Calculated

Federal income tax — the same 2026 IRS brackets (10%-37%) apply no matter which state you're in, with standard deductions of $16,100 (single), $32,200 (married filing jointly), or $24,150 (head of household).

FICA — 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (+0.9% above $200,000), also identical nationwide.

State income tax — this is where paychecks diverge. Nine states (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming) charge $0. About a dozen more use a single flat rate. The rest use progressive brackets. A few states (CA, NY) also add small mandatory payroll deductions like SDI or Paid Family Leave. Eight states now have a local/municipal tax selector on their own page: NY (NYC, Yonkers) and PA (Philadelphia) fully cover their local tax; MO (Kansas City, St. Louis) and AL (6 cities) cover every jurisdiction that applies; OH, IN, KY, and MD cover only their largest cities/counties — smaller jurisdictions in those four states, and every municipality in the other 42 states, still aren't computed. Each state page states exactly what is and isn't covered rather than silently omitting it.
