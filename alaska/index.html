How to Calculate Your Alaska Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Alaska charges no state income tax — your paycheck stops there. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Alaska resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. Alaska state tax: No state income tax.
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $10,592.5 = $54,407.5 (≈$4,533.96/month)

Alaska Paycheck Calculator FAQ

Does Alaska have a state income tax?

No. Alaska is one of nine states with no personal income tax. Only federal income tax and FICA are withheld from your paycheck.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Alaska?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Alaska takes home roughly $54,407.5/year (about $4,533.96/month) after federal tax, FICA.

Why doesn't Alaska have a state income tax?

Alaska is constitutionally or statutorily barred from levying a personal income tax on wages. See the Methodology section below for the specific citation.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Alaska Department of Revenue's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.

Methodology & Source

Alaska state tax figures sourced from Alaska Department of Revenue (https://tax.alaska.gov/), citing Alaska repealed its personal income tax in 1980. Federal brackets, standard deductions (single/MFJ/HoH), and FICA constants sourced from the IRS (Revenue Procedure 2025-32; married-filing-jointly Additional Medicare threshold of $250,000 is a separate, unindexed statutory figure). Alaska's married-filing-jointly and head-of-household figures are not yet independently verified and currently fall back to single-filer brackets — see the caveat in the formula section above.

This is an estimate based on standard single-filer federal, FICA, and AK state calculations. Actual withholding may differ based on your W-4 elections, pre-tax deductions, and other factors.
