How to Calculate Your New Jersey Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract New Jersey's $1,000 personal exemption from gross wages, then apply the 7-bracket state schedule (1.40%-10.75%) marginally.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, New Jersey resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. New Jersey state tax: Marginal tax on $64,000 taxable income (after $1,000 deduction) = $2,043.5
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $12,636 = $52,364 (≈$4,363.67/month)

New Jersey Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What are New Jersey's income tax brackets for 2026?

New Jersey uses 7 marginal brackets ranging from 1.40% to 10.75%.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in New Jersey?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in New Jersey takes home roughly $52,364/year (about $4,363.67/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax.

What deductions does New Jersey apply before taxing my income?

New Jersey applies a $1,000 personal exemption, before its marginal bracket schedule. See the "How New Jersey Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and New Jersey state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check New Jersey Division of Taxation's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.

Methodology & Source

New Jersey state tax figures sourced from New Jersey Division of Taxation (https://www.nj.gov/treasury/taxation/), citing N.J. Stat. § 54A:2-1 — 7-bracket schedule. Federal brackets and FICA constants sourced from the IRS (Revenue Procedure 2025-32).

This is an estimate based on standard single-filer federal, FICA, and NJ state calculations. Actual withholding may differ based on your W-4 elections, pre-tax deductions, and other factors. New Jersey has no standard deduction, only a $1,000 personal exemption. New Jersey SDI/FLI (small mandatory payroll deductions, typically well under 1% in recent years) are not modeled here.
