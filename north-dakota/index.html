How to Calculate Your North Dakota Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract North Dakota's $16,100 standard deduction from gross wages, then apply the 2-bracket state schedule (1.95%-2.50%) marginally.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, North Dakota resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. North Dakota state tax: Marginal tax on $48,900 taxable income (after $16,100 deduction) = $976.51
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $11,569.01 = $53,430.99 (≈$4,452.58/month)

North Dakota Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What are North Dakota's income tax brackets for 2026?

North Dakota uses 2 marginal brackets ranging from 1.95% to 2.50%. Standard deduction: $16,100 for single filers.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in North Dakota?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in North Dakota takes home roughly $53,430.99/year (about $4,452.58/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax.

What deductions does North Dakota apply before taxing my income?

North Dakota applies a $16,100 standard deduction, before its marginal bracket schedule. See the "How North Dakota Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and North Dakota state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check North Dakota Office of State Tax Commissioner's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.
