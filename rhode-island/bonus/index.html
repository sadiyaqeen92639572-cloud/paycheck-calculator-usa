Rhode Island Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What are Rhode Island's income tax brackets for 2026?

Rhode Island uses 3 marginal brackets ranging from 3.75% to 5.99%. Standard deduction: $10,550 for single filers.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Rhode Island?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Rhode Island takes home roughly $52,541.87/year (about $4,378.49/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax.

What deductions does Rhode Island apply before taxing my income?

Rhode Island applies a $10,550 standard deduction, plus a $4,700 personal exemption, before its marginal bracket schedule. See the "How Rhode Island Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and Rhode Island state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Rhode Island Division of Taxation's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.

Methodology & Source

Rhode Island state tax figures sourced from Rhode Island Division of Taxation (https://tax.ri.gov/), citing R.I. Gen. Laws § 44-30-2.6. Federal brackets, standard deductions (single/MFJ/HoH), and FICA constants sourced from the IRS (Revenue Procedure 2025-32; married-filing-jointly Additional Medicare threshold of $250,000 is a separate, unindexed statutory figure). Rhode Island's married-filing-jointly and head-of-household figures are not yet independently verified and currently fall back to single-filer brackets — see the caveat in the formula section above.

This is an estimate based on standard single-filer federal, FICA, and RI state calculations. Actual withholding may differ based on your W-4 elections, pre-tax deductions, and other factors.
