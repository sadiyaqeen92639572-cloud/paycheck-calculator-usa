How to Calculate Your Massachusetts Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract Massachusetts's $4,400 personal exemption from gross wages, then apply the flat 5% state rate. An additional 4% surtax applies to income over $1,107,750.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Massachusetts resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. Massachusetts state tax: ($65,000 - $4,400 deduction) × 5.00% = $3,030
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $13,622.5 = $51,377.5 (≈$4,281.46/month)

Massachusetts Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What is Massachusetts's state income tax rate?

Massachusetts charges a flat 5% rate for tax year 2026.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Massachusetts?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Massachusetts takes home roughly $51,377.5/year (about $4,281.46/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax.

What deductions does Massachusetts apply before taxing my income?

Massachusetts applies a $4,400 personal exemption, plus an additional 4% surtax on income over $1,107,750, before the flat 5% rate. See the "How Massachusetts Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and Massachusetts state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Massachusetts Department of Revenue's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.
