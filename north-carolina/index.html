How to Calculate Your North Carolina Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single), apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to $184,500) + 1.45% Medicare (+0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract North Carolina's $12,750 single standard deduction from gross wages, then apply the flat 3.99% state rate.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay — North Carolina has no local income tax.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, North Carolina resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $65,000 × 7.65% = $4,972.50
  3. NC taxable wages: $65,000 - $12,750 = $52,250 → NC tax: $52,250 × 3.99% = $2,084.78
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $5,620 - $4,972.50 - $2,084.78 = $52,322.72 (≈$4,360.23/month)

North Carolina Paycheck Calculator FAQ

Is North Carolina's income tax rate still dropping?

Yes. Under Session Law 2023-134, North Carolina's flat rate has phased down annually — 4.5% (2024), 4.25% (2025), 3.99% (2026) — with further cuts to 3.75% (2027) and 3.5% (2028) scheduled if state revenue triggers are met.

What is North Carolina's standard deduction for 2026?

$12,750 for single filers under current law. A pending bill (S437) would raise it to $13,000, but that had not been enacted as of this page's last-verified date — check ncdor.gov for the final figure.

Does North Carolina have local income tax?

No. North Carolina counties and municipalities do not levy separate local income taxes on wages.
