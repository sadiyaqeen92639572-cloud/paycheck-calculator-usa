How to Calculate Your California Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single), apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to $184,500) + 1.45% Medicare (+0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract California's $5,706 standard deduction from gross wages, then apply the 9-bracket state schedule (1%-12.3%) marginally — plus 1% Mental Health Services Tax on income over $1,000,000.
  5. Subtract California SDI (1.3% of all wages, no cap in 2026) — a mandatory payroll deduction, not an income tax, but it reduces your paycheck.
  6. What's left is your net take-home pay.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, California resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $65,000 × 7.65% = $4,972.50
  3. CA taxable income: $65,000 - $5,706 = $59,294 → marginal CA tax = $2,203.76
  4. CA SDI: $65,000 × 1.3% = $845
  5. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $5,620 - $4,972.50 - $2,203.76 - $845 = $51,358.74 (≈$4,279.90/month)

California Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What are California's income tax brackets for 2026?

California uses 9 marginal brackets from 1% (income up to $10,756) to 12.3% (over $721,314 for single filers), plus a 1% Mental Health Services Tax on taxable income over $1,000,000, for a 13.3% top effective rate.

What is California SDI and is it a tax?

State Disability Insurance (SDI) is a mandatory payroll deduction, not a state income tax — 1.3% of all wages in 2026 with no wage cap. It funds short-term disability and Paid Family Leave benefits and is withheld separately from income tax.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in California?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in California takes home roughly $50,942/year (about $4,245/month) after federal tax, FICA, state income tax, and SDI.
