How to Calculate Your Alabama Take-Home Pay

  1. Start with your gross annual salary.
  2. Subtract the 2026 federal standard deduction ($16,100 single) to get taxable income, then apply the 2026 federal brackets (10%-37%).
  3. Subtract FICA: 6.2% Social Security (up to the $184,500 wage base) + 1.45% Medicare (all wages, +0.9% above $200,000).
  4. Subtract Alabama's $3,000 standard deduction and the federal tax you paid from gross wages, then apply the 3-bracket state schedule (2%-5%) marginally.
  5. What's left is your net take-home pay before any local tax.

Example: $65,000/year gross salary, single filer, Alabama resident.

  1. Federal tax: $5,620
  2. FICA: $4,972.5
  3. Alabama state tax: Marginal tax on $56,380 taxable income (after $8,620 deduction incl. federal tax paid) = $2,779
  4. Net annual pay: $65,000 - $13,371.5 = $51,628.5 (≈$4,302.38/month)

Alabama Paycheck Calculator FAQ

What are Alabama's income tax brackets for 2026?

Alabama uses 3 marginal brackets ranging from 2% to 5%. Standard deduction: $3,000 for single filers.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Alabama?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Alabama takes home roughly $51,628.5/year (about $4,302.38/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax — before any local tax.

What deductions does Alabama apply before taxing my income?

Alabama applies a $3,000 standard deduction, plus a deduction for federal income tax paid, before its marginal bracket schedule. See the "How Alabama Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and Alabama state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Alabama Department of Revenue's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.

Does this calculator include Alabama local or city income tax?

Some Alabama cities and counties (e.g. Birmingham, Bessemer) levy a local occupational tax, typically 0.5%-2% of wages, not computed here.
