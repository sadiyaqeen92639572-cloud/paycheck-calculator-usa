How Bonuses Are Taxed in Alabama (2026)

A bonus paid on a separate check in Alabama has a flat 22% federal tax withheld (37% on cumulative supplemental wages over $1,000,000 in a year), 7.65% FICA, and Alabama state income tax at your marginal rate. Withholding is not a higher tax rate — it is just how much your employer holds back up front; your actual tax on the bonus is settled when you file, and any over-withholding comes back as refund.

Some employers instead use the aggregate method — adding the bonus to your regular paycheck and withholding as if that were every check — which can withhold more or less than the flat 22%. The calculator above uses the flat-rate (percentage) method.

Alabama Bonus Tax FAQ

What are Alabama's income tax brackets for 2026?

Alabama uses 3 marginal brackets ranging from 2% to 5%. Standard deduction: $3,000 for single filers.

How much is take-home pay on $65,000 in Alabama?

On a $65,000 gross salary, a single filer in Alabama takes home roughly $51,628.5/year (about $4,302.38/month) after federal tax, FICA, and state tax — before any local tax.

What deductions does Alabama apply before taxing my income?

Alabama applies a $3,000 standard deduction, plus a deduction for federal income tax paid, before its marginal bracket schedule. See the "How Alabama Paycheck Tax Is Calculated" section above for the exact formula.

Does this estimate change if I'm married or have dependents?

This calculator uses single-filer figures only (v1 scope). Married filing jointly typically uses different federal and Alabama state brackets and a larger standard deduction — your actual take-home pay will differ. Dependents can also reduce federal withholding via the W-4. Check Alabama Department of Revenue's married-filing-jointly tables for a precise figure.

Does this calculator include Alabama local or city income tax?

Some Alabama cities and counties (e.g. Birmingham, Bessemer) levy a local occupational tax, typically 0.5%-2% of wages, not computed here.

Methodology & Source

Alabama state tax figures sourced from Alabama Department of Revenue (https://www.revenue.alabama.gov/), citing Ala. Code § 40-18-5 — 3-bracket schedule; § 40-18-15 permits deducting federal income tax paid. Federal brackets, standard deductions (single/MFJ/HoH), and FICA constants sourced from the IRS (Revenue Procedure 2025-32; married-filing-jointly Additional Medicare threshold of $250,000 is a separate, unindexed statutory figure). Alabama's married-filing-jointly and head-of-household figures are not yet independently verified and currently fall back to single-filer brackets — see the caveat in the formula section above.

Local tax figures sourced from City occupational/license tax ordinances (Birmingham, Bessemer, Gadsden, Attalla, Opelika, Auburn). Rates cross-checked against two independent secondary sources 2026-07-29 (city-tax aggregator listings and payroll-guide summaries); Gadsden's own city revenue page does not publish the numeric rate online, so it relies on secondary sourcing only — flagged here rather than silently treated as equally verified. Alabama has dozens of smaller municipalities with occupational tax not listed below; select "Other" for those.

Bonus federal withholding uses the flat supplemental-wage rate (22%, 37% above $1,000,000 cumulative supplemental wages/year), per IRS Publication 15 (Circular E), Employer's Tax Guide, 2026 edition — the alternative "aggregate method" is not modeled. Alabama publishes no separate supplemental withholding rate, so the calculator estimates the state tax on the bonus from your marginal bracket.

This is an estimate based on standard single-filer federal, FICA, and AL state calculations. Actual withholding may differ based on your W-4 elections, pre-tax deductions, and other factors. Alabama's standard deduction is income-graduated ($2,000-$4,000 for single filers depending on AGI); this estimate uses a flat $3,000 approximation.
